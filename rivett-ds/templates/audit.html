A frank, single-pass audit

The site is good. It is not yet good for the list you are about to send it to.

An external read of rivett.tech, scored against the 19,761-row LinkedIn prospect list — Nick Huber's universe of US SMB operators in self-storage, real estate, construction, accounting, HVAC, plumbing, and professional services.

ScopeLive homepage, /diagnostic, /revenue-leak-mri, design-spec, tokens, voice docs
AudienceTier-1 prospects, n=2,400+ in May cohort
Reviewed byExternal
Read time14 min
TL;DR

The brand system is the strongest thing here. Editorial newspaper aesthetic, sage and forest accents, the dot mark, the italic Newsreader as whisper — this is the most defensible asset on the site, and it does the heavy lifting your prospects' agencies cannot.

The positioning, the pricing, and the funnel shape (free MRI → fixed diagnostic → scoped build) are also strong, and notably more honest than 95% of what your list is being pitched.

The gap is audience-specific evidence. There are zero testimonials, zero case studies, zero client logos, zero numbers from a real engagement. For cold LinkedIn traffic from a list this skeptical, that is the conversion-killer. Not the design. Not the copy. The proof.

Plus three secondary issues: the live H1 has drifted into consultant-jargon ("growth infrastructure") for an audience that speaks in jobs booked and phone rings; the design-spec files in the repo are now stale relative to the deployed site; and the conversion ladder leads with a $3,500 ask when it should lead with the free MRI for cold traffic.

01 · Audience fit

The list says "phone rings." The H1 says "growth infrastructure."

Three personas dominate the prospect list. The site speaks fluently to one of them, partially to the second, and barely registers with the third.

The May cohort skews heavily toward Nick Huber's "boring profitable un-glamorous" universe: owner-operators of 5–500 person service businesses, with a heavy real-estate and trades cluster on top. They are tagged across services like AdRhino, BoldSEO, RE Cost Seg, Shepherd, Titan Risk, and the CEO Bootcamp — meaning they have already bought (or considered) downstream Nick services. They are pre-qualified for the offer. They are not pre-qualified for the language.

P-01 · The trades operator

HVAC, plumbing, paving, glass, packaging.

N in list~6,400

Owner or president of a 4–35 person shop. Spends $5–40k/mo on Google + LSAs. Wary of consultants, allergic to AI hype. Reads every word of his estimator's invoices.

"I don't need growth infrastructure. I need the phone to ring on Tuesdays."

P-02 · The real-estate operator

Self-storage, multifamily, REPE, brokerage.

N in list~5,100

Founder, principal, or VP at a 10–200 person operator. Already lives inside Nick's content world. Buys frameworks, then resells them as IP. Most aligned audience for the current site language.

"I get this. Send me the memo. What do you charge?"

P-03 · The mid-market c-suite

Microsoft, Colliers, Matthews, GEE Group.

N in list~1,800

VP, director, or chief-of-staff inside a 200–23,000 person org. Tagged into Nick's content for personal interest, not buying intent. Will not hire a one-person practice for their employer.

"Interesting. Sending to my CMO, who will not call you."

F.01.1
Critical

The site has zero testimonials. Not one.

The design spec in your repo had a placeholder slot (.quote-slot with a sage callout) that was removed from the deployed site, not filled. The "By the numbers" table — career, managed spend, continents — is input-side proof. Operators want output-side proof.

Even one quote, even ungated and unattributed by name, is worth more than the $100M figure. "Fred re-built our paid in three weeks. Cost-per-booked-job dropped 41%. We stopped paying our previous agency the month after." — Owner, 14-truck HVAC company, Texas. That is the unlock.

Currently on site: 0 testimonials, 0 logos, 0 named case studies, 0 before/after numbers.

F.01.2
Critical

The Somewhere.com tell is buried.

For your prospect list, "ex-growth at Somewhere.com" is the single highest-credibility credential on the page. Nick's audience already knows that brand and the philosophy behind it. Right now it appears twice in the body copy of section 01 and section 04.

Move it into the hero. "Fifteen years inside paid acquisition. Most recently ex-growth at Somewhere.com." as a stamp directly under the H1. Same caliber of credential, three seconds earlier in the read.

"Self-storage · Brokerage · REPE · Property mgmt · Contractors · Agencies · Professional services · Wholesale" only appears below the fold of section 5 ("Who this is for"). For a cold visitor from a LinkedIn DM, that is too late — most prospects bounce inside 25 seconds.

Surface a two-line industry strip in the meta band under the hero ("works with: self-storage, multifamily, brokerage, contractors, agencies, professional services") so the trades operator sees themselves before deciding to scroll.

PriorityP1
F.01.4
Material

Persona-3 is currently a click-and-bounce. Not a problem to solve, a budget choice to confirm.

The site is correctly tuned for a one-person practice working with operators 50–500 people. The mid-market c-suite slice of the list (~1,800 names) is going to land, read, recognize this is not for them, and not refer it onward because there is no shareable artifact.

Either accept the bounce, or give them one shareable thing — a Field Note titled like "Why your CMO is selling you the busywork," with an inline "forward this to your CMO" CTA — that converts the bounce into reach.

PriorityP2
02 · Brand & design system

The system is the moat. It is also where the discipline is leaking.

The strongest asset on the site, by a clear margin. Token rigour, voice rules, and editorial type pairing are unusually disciplined for a single-operator practice. Two real bugs hiding inside the rigour.

What is working. The newspaper-meets-trade-journal aesthetic — square corners, hairline rules, mono labels with the 8px green dot, italic Newsreader as the editorial whisper, and the navy-on-warm-paper palette — is genuinely distinctive. None of your competitor agencies look like this. Most look like Linear, Vercel, or a Webflow template.

The voice rules in docs/voice.md ("no em-dashes, replaced by the period; no emoji; lowercase wordmark; posts are notes, not essays; author byline is Fred Style") are the kind of constraints that compound into a recognisable house style. Keep them.

The dot mark is doing real work. It is the only ornament. That is exactly right.

What is leaking. Two real token bugs and one structural one.

Bug 1. src/styles/tokens.css defines --accent: #8FBF3F and --accent-deep: #4A6E18. The three files in design-spec/ redeclare these inline as #6FB582 and #3F7553. Two greens are in production. The README claims tokens are the single source of truth — they are not. Pick one set; delete the inline overrides.

Bug 2. --ink-faint is #5F6B7D in tokens but #7A8597 in spec files. The spec value fails AA contrast on --paper at body sizes — anything labelled "ink-faint" inside body text is undercooked.

Bug 3. README says "design-spec is canonical, replicate exactly." The deployed homepage has 6 sections; the spec has 5. Either re-spec or remove the contract.

F.02.1
Material

Two greens in production.

Forensic finding above. Pick one accent pair and delete the inline overrides in design-spec/*.html. If the spec files are reference-only, the README must say so; if they are canonical, they must read from tokens.css.

PriorityP1
F.02.2
Strength

Italic Newsreader as the brand whisper. Hold this line.

Pairing a tight modern grotesk with a single italic serif used only for the second clause of a thought is the sharpest typographic decision on the site. It is the visual analogue of "two short sentences instead of one long one." Resist the future temptation to use Newsreader roman, or Newsreader at small sizes. The whisper is the value.

PriorityKeep
F.02.3
Material

The sage callout colour is underused.

You introduced --sage #E8F0D9 and .numbox for stat blocks, then only deploy them inside blog posts. The homepage has none. A single sage stat panel after section 01 ("$100M+ managed across 4 continents · ex-Somewhere.com · 15 yrs") would carry weight that the current dim-grey numbers row does not.

PriorityP2
F.02.4
Strength

Square corners. Hairlines. No emoji. Compounds into seriousness.

--r-0: 0 as the default radius is the right call for this practice. Every rounded-corner SaaS template is competing in a different market. The square-corner discipline reads as "operator, not agency" without saying it.

PriorityKeep
03 · Copy & positioning

The best line on the site is hidden in section 03. The H1 is the sixth-best line on the site.

The voice is largely intact. The hierarchy of which line earns the largest type is mis-tuned for cold traffic.

Run the homepage through the senior-operator-at-1am test (does this read like a person who has done the work, or a marketer pretending to?). Most of it passes. The H1 fails. The deck below the H1 is closer. The line buried in section 03 is best of all.

As deployed

Growth infrastructure for operators.

Reads like Notion-buyer copy. "Infrastructure" is a category for engineers. The trades persona will not recognise themselves.
Candidate · option A

I find where the money is leaking. Then I fix it.

First-person. Concrete verbs (find, fix). The italic clause does the work the voice rules describe. Mirrors the Revenue MRI tool name without naming it.
Currently buried · § 03

"Most agencies are now selling the busywork as the product. I am selling senior judgment, with the busywork automated underneath. Different deal."

The strongest single passage on the entire site. Three sentences. Names a competitor pattern. States the offer. Ends on a closer. It is currently in the third paragraph of section three.
Candidate · option B

Fifteen years running paid. Now selling the judgment, not the busywork.

Surfaces the buried line as the H1 itself. Anchors on the only thing operators care about: the years. Italic clause carries the deal.
F.03.1
Critical

"Growth infrastructure" is a category, not a hook.

For the real-estate persona, it parses. For the trades persona, it triggers the consultant-allergy. For the c-suite persona, it triggers a different consultant-allergy. The H1 is the one line every visitor reads — it cannot afford to be category copy.

PriorityP0
F.03.2
Material

"Operating layer" / "judgment layer" / "below the judgment layer." Three layers, one cold prospect.

The "judgment vs busywork" distinction is the right one. The "layer" framing is a tic — it appears six times across the homepage and the diagnostic page. Cut it to two. Use "the work" or "the system" for the rest.

PriorityP1
F.03.3
Strength

"Probably not" anti-fit list on the diagnostic page.

"You want a junior to take orders. I do not run that way." is the kind of sentence that converts on its own. Most consulting sites cannot bring themselves to disqualify anyone. This page does. Replicate the pattern on the homepage as a one-liner under section 05.

PriorityKeep
F.03.4
Material

"ex-growth" is in-group jargon. Spell it once.

For Nick's audience, "ex-growth at Somewhere.com" is recognisable but ambiguous (ex-employee? former growth lead? ex-CGO?). On first reference, write "led growth at Somewhere.com." After that, the shorthand earns its keep.

PriorityP2
04 · IA & conversion

The funnel is right. The first rung is too tall for the list.

A free MRI, a $3,500 fixed diagnostic, a scoped build, and a monthly run is exactly the right ladder for a senior practice. The order in which the homepage presents those rungs is wrong for cold LinkedIn traffic.

F.04.1
Critical

The primary CTA on a cold visit should be the MRI, not the diagnostic.

Top nav and hero both lead with "Book diagnostic →" (a $3,500 fixed-price ask). The Revenue MRI is positioned as a parallel option but visually subordinated. For a prospect arriving from a LinkedIn DM who has never met you, the conversion ladder needs a $0 first rung. Promote MRI to primary; demote diagnostic to secondary.

Keep the diagnostic primary on warm-traffic pages (footer, /diagnostic, /blog post end-CTAs). Cold homepage traffic is a different audience.

PriorityP0
F.04.2
Critical

Two dead links on /diagnostic.

"Book a kick-off call →" and "Open the calendar →" both resolve to # in the deployed page. For a $3,500 conversion page, these need to be wired to a real Cal.com slot before any LinkedIn outreach goes out.

PriorityP0
F.04.3
Material

Two redundant proof strips above the fold.

The hero has both a left-column "meta" row (Principal / Spend run / Markets / Proof) and a right-column "hero-r" sidebar (M01 price, M02 status, M03 status, workhours, reply window). They overlap in function — both are "trust microcopy." Pick one. The right sidebar is more useful (concrete: price, availability, reply time); the left meta band can collapse into a single line under the H1.

PriorityP1
F.04.4
Material

The MRI tool gates the readout behind nine inputs.

Spend, leads, deal value, agency cost, response time, lead-to-opp, opp-close, internal hours, hourly cost. That is a real-money commitment of attention before any payoff. Either (a) show a partial readout from the first three inputs (spend, leads, deal value) and gate the rest, or (b) ship a 60-second "express" version that estimates the leak from spend + leads alone.

The current full version stays — it is a great qualified-lead capture for warm traffic. The express version becomes the cold-traffic on-ramp.

PriorityP1
F.04.5
Material

No newsletter capture on Field Notes.

30+ notes is a real archive. None of the routes through the site lead to a "subscribe" primary CTA — a missed compounding asset, especially given the prospect list will mostly read once and forget. Add a quiet inline subscribe at the end of every note, "Get new Field Notes by email. No newsletter trap."

PriorityP1
F.04.6
Material

Section order on the homepage de-prioritises industry fit.

Current order: Hero → Track record → Modules → AI → Cost arbitrage → Who → CTA. Three of the four highest-converting sections for cold traffic (industry fit, pricing, anti-fit) are below sections that argue philosophy. Suggested order: Hero → Modules (with prices) → Who → Track record → Cost arbitrage → AI → CTA. Lead with "what does it cost and is this for me," follow with credibility.

Closing

The brand will hold up. The send will not, until the proof is on the page.

Of the twelve items above, three are non-negotiable before any outreach to the May cohort: one testimonial, the MRI as primary CTA, and the two dead links wired. None of those are design problems. They are go-to-market hygiene.

Everything else can wait for a v02 sprint. The site is a credible front door. It is not yet a closing surface for a 19,000-row list of skeptical SMB operators. The fastest path from the first to the second is named, output-side proof — even one piece of it.

The system you have built is rare. Hold the line on it. Then ship a quote.
