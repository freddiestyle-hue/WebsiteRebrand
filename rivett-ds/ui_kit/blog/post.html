Cadence over volume.

Why shipping less, but instrumented, beats shipping more. The fortnightly read-out, the green-light decision, and the budget that follows.

By TR 2025·09·14 07 min read 02 vitals updated

TL · DR

The fortnightly read-out is the unit of work. Two weeks of shipping. One memo. One decision. Anything faster is theatre. Anything slower is a meeting.

Most growth practices ask the wrong question. They ask how much did we ship this week? — and the answer is always a number that goes up, because shipping is easy and instrumenting is hard. The right question is the one that takes longer. Did the curve bend?

The right unit for that question is the fortnight. Two weeks is long enough that you have signal. It’s short enough that you haven’t spent £20k on a hunch. It’s the only cadence I have ever seen survive a senior operator’s honest review.

02The fortnight

On a Tuesday morning, before the standup, the read-out lands. It is a single document. It opens with the diagnosis: green, amber, red, insufficient, per vital. It closes with the decision row: halt, hold, proceed.

The middle is the working. The middle is where most read-outs fail — they get long, they get charty, they get nice to have sections. The middle is also where the discipline lives: write a sentence per vital, write the number, write the verdict, move on.

03The decision row

The hardest part of the memo is the last row. It says, in three words, what changes on Wednesday. The rule is: every memo lands with a decision. No exceptions. If you cannot say we are halting X or we are doubling Y by the end of the page, the memo is not finished — or the work is not finished.

"A memo without a decision is a meeting. A meeting without a decision is a stand-up."
— field note 04 · 2025·09

04What we halted

The halt list is the closest thing the practice has to a brag. The brag is not we shipped 41 experiments this quarter. The brag is we halted 17 of them after the first read. The cost of the halt is the meeting that did not happen. The value is the next 90 days.

05Field rules

  1. One number per memo. The lede. Everything else supports it.
  2. Fortnightly or nothing. Weekly is theatre. Monthly is a board meeting.
  3. Decisions in the last row. Halt / hold / proceed. No fourth.
  4. Halt count is the brag. Not ship count.
  5. The memo is short. If it’s longer than two screens, it is hiding.

— TR, Cape Town. 2025·09
