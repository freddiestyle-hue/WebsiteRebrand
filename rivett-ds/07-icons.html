Design system/07 Icons
Section 07 · A small, opinionated set

Twenty-four marks. Not a pictogram for every noun.

A 24-grid set built on a 1.6 stroke, square caps, mitre joins. The accent dot earns its place — one per icon, only where it carries meaning. Six core, six functional, six editorial, six metric. No twenty-fifth without a reviewer.

07 · 1Anatomy

One grid. One stroke. One dot.

Every icon is drawn inside a 24×24 viewBox, on a 1px sub-grid, with a 2px keyline padding all four sides. Stroke is 1.6, caps are square, joins are mitred, fills are off. The accent dot — when used — is 1.6px radius, placed on intersection or endpoint, never floating.

07 · 2Library

Twenty-four icons, in four families.

Core handles the brand's vocabulary — the modules and the verbs that recur on every page. Functional handles UI furniture. Editorial handles the blog. Metric handles the MRI. If you want a 25th, it must replace a member of its family.

07 · 3Sizes

Five sizes. No others.

Stroke does not scale. The icon redraws at each step so it survives the size. At 12px and below, icons are simplified — the inner detail and the dot may drop. Above 48px, the icon stops being an icon and becomes a graphic; use a different element.

12 pxinline meta · stroke 2.0
16 pxbuttons · table cells
24 pxlist rows · nav
36 pxmodule heads · default
48 pxfeature cards · max
07 · 4Treatment

Four treatments. Pick by surface.

Default is ink stroke on paper. The dot stays accent across every treatment except for status — confirm uses accent, decline uses signal-halt, halt uses signal-warn. Do not invent a fifth treatment.

07 · 5In use

Where they live. Where they don't.

Icons live in five contexts and nowhere else: module heads, the automate list, the MRI rubric, status meta in tables, and the inline arrow at the end of a CTA. They do not live in the hero, in body prose, in audience cards, or as decoration in margins.

07 · 6Misuse

What kills the set. Don't do these.

The icons feel like a single hand because they all obey the same rules. Every misuse below has a real-world example from a previous draft. They have all been ripped out.

Rounded caps. Lose the corners.
Stroke too thick. Use 1.6 only.
Filled silhouette. Stroke only.
Two dots. Maximum is one.
All-green icon. Stroke is ink.
Gradient backplate. None.
Inside body prose. ✗
Rotated. Icons are upright.
07 · 7Expansion

Adding a 25th. The bar is high.

The set deliberately refuses one-to-one coverage of the product. Reach for an existing icon first; the second-best fit is almost always the right answer. If you must add, the new mark replaces an existing member of its family — the headcount stays at 24.

StepRule
01 · JustifyWrite a one-sentence reason it can't reuse an existing icon. If the sentence uses nice to have, stop.
02 · FamilyAssign to core, functional, editorial, or metric. Identify the member it will replace.
03 · Draw24-grid. 1.6 stroke. Square caps, mitre joins. 2-unit keyline padding. One dot maximum, accent only, on intersection or endpoint.
04 · TestRender at 12, 16, 24, 36, 48. If 12px loses the meaning, simplify the 12px version. Do not thicken the 36.
05 · Sit itPlace beside diagnostic, stack, and cadence. If it looks louder or rounder, redraw.
06 · Sign offReviewer is TR. Commit message icons: add ic-NN · NAME · replaces ic-NN · NAME. Update icons.svg and the inventory above.
